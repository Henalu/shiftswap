## Design Context

### Users
ShiftSwap is an internal web app for company employees who need to publish shifts, browse available swaps, express interest, negotiate with coworkers, and complete confirmation or signature steps when required.

Users are not visiting for inspiration or brand storytelling. They are usually trying to complete an operational task quickly, with low cognitive load, clear states, and obvious next actions. Mobile usability matters because employees may check and manage swaps from their phones.

### Brand Personality
The product should feel clean, efficient, trustworthy, modern, professional, friendly, and highly legible.

The emotional goal is confidence: users should feel that the product is reliable, easy to scan, and practical to use during day-to-day scheduling decisions.

### Aesthetic Direction
This should look like a modern internal workforce or scheduling product, not a marketing site and not an editorial experience.

Visual references:
- Stripe for cleanliness, visual system quality, structure, and polish
- Deputy as a mental model for a practical scheduling/workforce product
- Awwwards only as a benchmark for care and quality, not as a direct stylistic model

The target balance is:
- 70% functional clarity
- 20% modern polish
- 10% visual personality

The interface should be minimal without feeling cold, professional without feeling stiff, and modern without becoming decorative or experimental.

### Design Principles
1. Prioritize speed of understanding over novelty.
2. Make scanning effortless through strong hierarchy, spacing, and typography.
3. Use color to communicate meaning such as status, warning, success, and pending states.
4. Keep navigation and actions obvious so users can move quickly through the flow.
5. Reduce clutter and visual noise while preserving warmth and approachability.
